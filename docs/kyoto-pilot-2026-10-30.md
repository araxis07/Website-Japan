The existing groups remain **Kiyomizu-dera + Gion**, and **Fushimi Inari Taisha + Kyoto National Museum**. These are planning groups, not physically validated routes or a promise that every stop can be visited in one day.

## Approach references and map handoff

- **Kiyomizu-dera:** the [temple access page](https://www.kiyomizudera.or.jp/access.php) identifies Keihan Kiyomizu-Gojo and an approximately 25-minute walk. It specifies the Nio-mon approach via Kiyomizu-zaka and the emergency-road approach via Chawan-zaka, and warns that map apps can suggest routes that do not enter the grounds. The [temple FAQ](https://www.kiyomizudera.or.jp/en/faq/) describes wheelchair access; a station exit and the continuous street approach remain unverified here.
- **Gion:** Exit 7 is a station lift location, not the entrance to the district. Follow the existing [Southern Gion public-street/private-property guidance](https://kyoto.travel/en/responsible-travel/gion-manner-message-from-southern-gionmachi/). No accessible street circuit is inferred.
- **Kyoto National Museum:** [Getting Here](https://www.kyohaku.go.jp/eng/visit/access/) identifies Keihan Shichijo and the eastward walk along Shichijo Street; [Accessibility](https://www.kyohaku.go.jp/eng/visit/accessibility/) identifies the South Gate ramp. The connection from a particular station exit to that gate remains unverified.
- **Fushimi Inari:** [Access](https://inari.jp/en/access/) distinguishes JR Nara Line Inari from Keihan Main Line Fushimi-inari; [Map](https://inari.jp/en/map/) places Romon on the lower grounds. Neither source is treated as verification of the station exit, a continuous accessible route or summit access.

Pins, coordinates, Japanese search queries and stable IDs did not change. Google Maps still resolves the existing textual destination; a venue reference pin does not verify its selected entrance. Device-origin, previous-stop and lodging-origin handoffs, walking/transit results and the route's actual gate require manual acceptance. No new photographs are needed for these source-backed changes; existing entrance photographs remain historical landmark references.
